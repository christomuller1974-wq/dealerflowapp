Double Cab vs SuperCab: Choosing a Used Bakkie

Understand the differences between double-cab and extended-cab bakkies for passengers, tools, luggage and daily use.

Passenger space and access

Double cabs generally provide four conventional side doors and a larger rear passenger area. SuperCab, extended-cab and extra-cab layouts vary by manufacturer and model; rear seating and door access can be limited. Inspect the actual vehicle rather than relying on the name.

Load-bin space and payload

An extended-cab bakkie may offer a longer load bin than a comparable double cab, but dimensions and legal payload depend on the exact variant. Measure the load bin and verify the vehicle’s permitted gross and towing masses.

Family use versus work use

For regular rear-seat passengers, inspect seatbelts, child-seat compatibility, legroom and door access. For work, assess security of tools, canopy fit, load-bin length and ease of loading.

Test the specific model

Check the service history, clutch or automatic gearbox, suspension, tyres and signs of heavy towing or overloading. Compare insurance, fuel, servicing and resale expectations without assuming either cab type is always cheaper.
